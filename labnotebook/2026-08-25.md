# 2026-08-25

The plan for today is to attempt to run an agent, without any neurons, on the triple-t track with some sort of task that represents a similair task to the olson 2017 paper. 

## Task details 

Self-paced running task from a single start site through 3 turns to 1 or 4 food sites. Rat loops back to start through perimeter. Rat performs this task up to 80 times per session with even spread throughout reward centers. 

- Two 30 min free exloration periods to habituate. 
- 3-4 weeks of training for shaping, and trial and error training untill 80% were bsallistic (uninterrupted runs between start to reward without backtracking or stopping).
- After success there were additional training sessions on the rotated triple t, but not extensive training, just for familairty. 
- Rat undergoes surgery. Followed by another week of training post recovery.
- Task: From the start at the midpoint of a long edge, into the maze, straight sections interleaved with three left or right turns, turns at 51, 87, and 118 cm, ending at a goal at 140 cm.
- Reward rules: 
  - All-but-repeats (rat NS14): rewarded at any goal except the one just visited.
  - Visit-all (NS15 and NS16): rewarded at every goal, but rewards only reset after all four have been visited.
- Session details:
  - Standard session about 45 minutes: 3 to 10 minutes in the arena first, then about 80 rewarded runs. The arena time itself has two epochs of roughly 5 minutes each: cued straight crossings of the full arena for a quarter Cheerio at the track edge, orientations varied to sample the surface and produce track-like running, then free foraging for scattered crumbs.
  - Rotation sessions run 80 runs, a 5 minute water break while the track is wiped and rotated, then 80 more, about 1 hour 15 total

## Synthetic generation
We will probably want to use real trajectory data to simulate firing along a path. 

option 1: import_trajectory. You hand it times and positions from anywhere, it interpolates, and cells fire along the path. This is the standard method both for real tracking data and for scripted behavior, and it is what I recommend for us.
option 2: drift_velocity. Each update can take a drift vector that pulls the agent, blended with the random motion. This is the built-in way to steer an agent toward a goal. A middle option for us: our waypoints provide the pull, RatInABox physics adds jitter and wall handling. It might look more ratlike than pure scripting, or it might just fight the 8 cm corridors. Only a test settles it.

The pipeline, in order. Raw files never get modified and never enter the repo, everything derived from them is regenerable from raw plus code plus config.

### Suggested pipeline
1. Inventory. Get one file and look at it before writing anything. Document the format, units, sampling rate, coordinate system, what a session contains, how epochs are marked. Notebook entry. Everything downstream depends on this, so no code until one file has been seen.

2. Ingest. A loader in src that reads that format. Raw files sit outside the repo, pointed to by a local untracked path. If the files bundle spikes with tracking, and they probably do, the loader splits them at the door: behavior comes through, spike data goes to a separate location that only the validate package may read. This is the moment real subiculum spikes touch your filesystem, so the hard wall becomes a code path instead of a principle. Decision entry.

3. Clean. Tracking data has dropouts, reflection glitches, and LED swaps. Remove impossible jumps with a speed threshold, interpolate short gaps, exclude long gaps instead of inventing data, smooth lightly. All thresholds in a config, cleaning deterministic, per-session QC stats logged.

4. Derive. Position is the mean of the two LEDs, heading is the angle of the vector between them, exactly as the paper did. That heading is measured head direction, not travel direction, which is a real upgrade over the synthetic arm.

5. Register. Camera coordinates onto our room frame: scale, translation, rotation, maybe a mirror flip. Use the track itself as the target, the occupancy map traces the maze skeleton and gets fit to the centerline. Side effect worth expecting: this is where my inferred triple-T layout gets corrected by reality, and the config updates from the data. Also segment each session into epochs, arena, track, rotated track, dark, and label env per timestep.

6. Resample. 60 Hz down to our dt of 0.05. Interpolate the heading as a vector, not as a raw angle, so wraparound doesn't corrupt it. Then derive velocity, speed, and angular velocity with the same shared function the synthetic arm uses, so both arms are differentiated and smoothed identically.

7. Emit the shared schema. Same trajectory format as synthetic, plus provenance: source, rat, session, env hash, transform used, cleaning config hash, raw file checksum. Lands in the gitignored data directory like any other session.

8. QC report. One auto-generated figure per session: registered trajectory over the env drawing, speed histogram against the 3 cm per s filter, detected runs and goal visit counts checked against the paper's numbers, and the distribution of heading minus travel direction, which directly measures the idealization we've been carrying.

Then three uses, cheapest first: calibrate the synthetic generator from measured speed, dwell, and choice statistics instead of guesses. Drive input cells along real trajectories through import_trajectory for the real-arm datasets. And at validation time, the matched comparison of model units against real neurons over identical behavior.
